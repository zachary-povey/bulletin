## Top stories

### DWP takes benefits and pensions portals offline after "cyber incident"

The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) took most of its public-facing online services down after detecting "unusual activity" on Thursday 26 November. The services were still offline on Friday evening. Ministers say regular payments are unaffected. Claimants reported cancelled appointments and, in some cases, missing payments. The government has not said whether the incident was an attack.

**Established facts**

- The government took the portal down late on Thursday. On Friday 27 November people could not check a state pension, apply for Pip or claim universal credit online, and the "Tell Us Once" bereavement service was also unavailable. [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/27/dwp-cyber-incident-pensions-benefits-systems-offline), [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c8wy5p6pv2e2o)
- Work and Pensions Secretary Pat McFadden called it "a serious cyber incident". He said there was "no evidence of anyone's personal details being compromised" and that regular payments were "continuing as normal". [Guardian live](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/live/2026/nov/27/department-work-pensions-offline-cyber-incident-rosebank-slapps-latest-news-updates), [Standard/PA](https://www.standard.co.uk/news/politics/pat-mcfadden-department-for-work-and-pensions-government-gchq-national-cyber-security-centre-b1306685.html)
- No 10 declined to rule out a hack. It said there was "no other evidence that a malicious actor" had targeted other departments. The DWP is working with GCHQ's National Cyber Security Centre and the Government Cyber Coordination Centre, and says claimants will not be sanctioned for missing online journal entries during the outage. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c8wy5p6pv2e2o), [Standard/PA](https://www.standard.co.uk/news/politics/pat-mcfadden-department-for-work-and-pensions-government-gchq-national-cyber-security-centre-b1306685.html)
- Internal DWP systems were restored on Friday, but the public portal remained down (single source). The Information Commissioner's Office says it is in contact with the department (single source). [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/27/dwp-cyber-incident-pensions-benefits-systems-offline), [LBC/PA](https://www.lbc.co.uk/article/9d616773563447ecaa242cdb5326164c-5HjdnfP_2/)

**Claimed or disputed**

- **Payments.** Several claimants told the BBC and the Guardian that money due on Friday had not arrived. PA was told that the reported delays are "not related" to the cyber incident. DWP insiders told the Guardian that tens of thousands of manual payments could be backed up over the weekend. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c8wy5p6pv2e2o), [LBC/PA](https://www.lbc.co.uk/article/9d616773563447ecaa242cdb5326164c-5HjdnfP_2/), [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/27/dwp-cyber-incident-pensions-benefits-systems-offline)
- **Cause.** David Knott, the government's chief technology officer until December 2025, told BBC Newsnight that his "strong suspicion is this is an attack". The government has not confirmed this. The Guardian notes that the incident follows a government warning of a higher risk of Russian sabotage, but no source attributes the incident to any actor. [LBC/PA](https://www.lbc.co.uk/article/9d616773563447ecaa242cdb5326164c-5HjdnfP_2/), [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/27/dwp-cyber-incident-pensions-benefits-systems-offline)

**Opinion and analysis**

- Dame Chi Onwurah (Labour), who chairs the Commons science committee, called it a "wake-up call" about weak digital infrastructure. Conservative shadow AI minister Ben Spencer said it showed that security "cannot be taken for granted". Debbie Abrahams, the Labour chair of the work and pensions committee, said taking systems offline was "absolutely the right thing to do". [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/27/dwp-cyber-incident-pensions-benefits-systems-offline), [Standard/PA](https://www.standard.co.uk/news/politics/pat-mcfadden-department-for-work-and-pensions-government-gchq-national-cyber-security-centre-b1306685.html)

**What next**: The government has given no date for restoring services. Benefit bills falling due on 1 December are the next pressure point for claimants.

### Rosebank: pro-drilling Labour MPs prepare a counter-letter

Following the anti-Rosebank letter, Labour MPs who back the North Sea oilfield are organising their own lobbying, and the Scottish Labour leader publicly supported the project. The decision rests formally with Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh.

**Established facts**

- 92 Labour MPs, including ministerial aides, signed a letter to the prime minister urging him not to allow the project. The letter warns that approval risks an "irreversible 'point of no return'" for Labour's climate credibility. Its organiser is Catherine Fookes, MP for Monmouthshire. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cw24ez32d1n4o), [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/26/labour-split-over-rosebank-oilfield-exposed-by-letter-from-nearly-100-mps), [STV/PA](https://news.stv.tv/politics/labour-mps-warn-burnham-not-to-approve-rosebank-oil-field)
- Scottish Labour leader Michael Marra backed Rosebank, citing the effect of North Sea decline on "working-class people in vocational jobs". Environment minister Emma Hardy told Sky News there are "different views in the Labour party". [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/27/rosebank-supporting-mps-warn-pm-blocking-the-oilfield-would-mean-lights-out-for-labour-in-scotland)
- Downing Street said the decision "will be taken by the energy secretary through a quasi-judicial process". [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/27/rosebank-supporting-mps-warn-pm-blocking-the-oilfield-would-mean-lights-out-for-labour-in-scotland), [STV/PA](https://news.stv.tv/politics/labour-mps-warn-burnham-not-to-approve-rosebank-oil-field)

**Claimed or disputed**

- **Supporters.** As many as 80 pro-drilling MPs are considering a rival letter, according to the Guardian and, as cited by City AM, Sky News. One MP told the Guardian that rejecting the project would mean "lights out for Labour in Scotland". [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/27/rosebank-supporting-mps-warn-pm-blocking-the-oilfield-would-mean-lights-out-for-labour-in-scotland), [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/labour-factions-battle-rosebank-approval/)
- **More opponents.** The Times reported that a further 50 MPs have privately lobbied No 10 against the project. This is reported here via HuffPost; the Times article was not read. Unnamed aides told the BBC they are "considering our positions". [HuffPost](https://www.huffingtonpost.co.uk/entry/andy-burnham-rosebank-jackdaw-rebellion_uk_6b0919f0e4b01f494a034d52), [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cw24ez32d1n4o)
- **Costs and benefits.** Supporters cite about 500 jobs and up to £500m a year in tax. The letter says the tax take would be "less than 0.1%" of annual revenue, and signatory Fleur Anderson says the OBR expects receipts to fall to nearly zero by 2030. Survation polling found 64% of Scots in favour and 16% against. [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/27/rosebank-supporting-mps-warn-pm-blocking-the-oilfield-would-mean-lights-out-for-labour-in-scotland), [STV/PA](https://news.stv.tv/politics/labour-mps-warn-burnham-not-to-approve-rosebank-oil-field)

**Opinion and analysis**

- Lord Stern, the climate economist, told PoliticsHome there is "little domestic benefit to set against the potential damage to world leadership". [PoliticsHome](https://www.politicshome.com/news/article/lord-stern-climate-report-author-warns-rosebank-must-not-go-ahead)
- In the left-leaning *Guardian*, Andy Beckett argues that Burnham's deliberate pace ("Andy time") may suit Westminster, but that on climate the government must "pick a side". [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/nov/27/andy-burnham-slow-politics-working-media-voters-reset-westminster)

**What next**: Talks on a pro-Rosebank joint letter continue over the weekend. A decision is expected within weeks.

### Badenoch makes leaving the ECHR and scrapping net zero conditions for Tory candidates

**Established facts**

- At the East Midlands Conservative conference in Newark on Friday 27 November, Kemi Badenoch said every Conservative candidate must sign a "candidates covenant". It commits them to leaving the European Convention on Human Rights, repealing the Climate Change Act and the net-zero target, staying out of the EU and shrinking the state. She said it would apply to shadow cabinet members too. Former chief whip Sir Mark Spencer, now the candidate for Newark, signed first. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cmwy95295835o), [Standard/PA](https://www.standard.co.uk/news/politics/kemi-badenoch-echr-mark-spencer-conservative-tobias-ellwood-b1306698.html)
- Badenoch said that "if any deal is signed with the European Union… that prevents us from leaving the ECHR, we will reverse that deal too". She denied that the covenant is a "purity test". [Standard/PA](https://www.standard.co.uk/news/politics/kemi-badenoch-echr-mark-spencer-conservative-tobias-ellwood-b1306698.html)

**Claimed or disputed**

- Former minister Tobias Ellwood said he had not passed "the purity tests" to stand again. Reform's Lee Anderson claimed that 25 sitting Tory MPs who have criticised leaving the ECHR will "bottle it". Lib Dem leader Ed Davey called the covenant a "Farage pact". [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cmwy95295835o), [Standard/PA](https://www.standard.co.uk/news/politics/kemi-badenoch-echr-mark-spencer-conservative-tobias-ellwood-b1306698.html)

**Opinion and analysis**

- Lord Barwell, Theresa May's former chief of staff, called the covenant "bad policy" and, given the seats the party is targeting, "bad politics". Greenpeace said it commits candidates to "climate chaos". [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cmwy95295835o), [Guardian live](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/live/2026/nov/27/department-work-pensions-offline-cyber-incident-rosebank-slapps-latest-news-updates)

### Government backs Tory MP's anti-Slapp bill

**Established facts**

- Sir John Whittingdale's private member's bill would extend protection against Slapps (lawsuits used to silence critics) beyond economic-crime cases. It would let judges dismiss abusive claims early and protect defendants from heavy legal costs. It received an unopposed second reading on Friday 27 November with government support. Courts minister Sarah Sackman said the justice system "should never be a weapon of intimidation". [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cw24ez2zvge0o), [Guardian live](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/live/2026/nov/27/department-work-pensions-offline-cyber-incident-rosebank-slapps-latest-news-updates)

**Claimed or disputed**

- The UK Anti-Slapp Coalition and the NUJ support the bill but say its early-dismissal threshold is "almost impossibly high". Whittingdale said he expects further talks with ministers (single source). [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cw24ez2zvge0o)

### First data on Burnham's rough-sleeping pledge

**Established facts**

- Government figures show at least 3,276 people sleeping rough were offered a route off the streets in October. England had an estimated 9,369 rough sleepers in September. July's figure of 9,798 was the highest since comparable records began in 2020. Burnham called it "early progress". About 63% of councils said they were confident of meeting the pledge by 1 December, and 14% were not. [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/society/2026/nov/27/third-rough-sleepers-offered-bed-support-burnham-christmas-vow), [Guardian live](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/live/2026/nov/27/department-work-pensions-offline-cyber-incident-rosebank-slapps-latest-news-updates)

**Claimed or disputed**

- Big Issue founder Lord Bird and Shelter criticised the absence of data on how many offers were refused or led to people moving off the street. Shelter said "nearly two-thirds" were still waiting for an offer (single source). [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/society/2026/nov/27/third-rough-sleepers-offered-bed-support-burnham-christmas-vow)
