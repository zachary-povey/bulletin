## Top stories

### EU treaty texts reveal a "participation fee" and no UK vote on rules

The treaty texts published on Thursday 26 November (covered in the previous update) contain more detail than the summary did. The UK will pay a surcharge on top of its share of EU running costs, and it gets a consultative role but no vote on EU rules. On Friday 27 November Scottish Secretary Douglas Alexander was due to become the first cabinet minister to say openly that the deal should be taken further.

**Established facts**

- The UK's main "operational contribution" to the EU agencies and systems behind the food-standards (SPS) and emissions-trading deals is set by the size of its economy relative to the EU's. On top of that the UK pays a "participation fee" of 4% of the main contribution. Officials expect the extra costs to be "in the low tens of millions" a year, though no overall estimate has been published (single source for the figures). [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/ck2057x387ego), [FT](https://www.ft.com/content/00e41702-b11f-4a28-9233-16ce4cff99c7) (feed summary only)
- Under the 80-page food agreement the UK will "dynamically align" with EU rules. It has a "consultative" role but "no voting rights", even in emergencies such as disease outbreaks, and the EU may bring in temporary protective measures if the two sides cannot agree on alignment. [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/26/uk-bill-to-implement-eu-deal-closer-future-ties), [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/ck2057x387ego)
- The Cutting Costs Bill is a framework bill: future "partnership treaties", such as a planned energy market deal, can be enacted through it. It rules out rejoining the single market, the customs union or the EU. The youth-mobility text, covering 100,000 visas, has not yet been published and will be linked to the food deal. [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/26/uk-bill-to-implement-eu-deal-closer-future-ties)
- Cabinet Office minister Hamish Falconer wrote to Kemi Badenoch and Nigel Farage challenging them to back the bill: "A vote against this bill is a vote to maintain costly bureaucracy." (single source) [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/26/uk-bill-to-implement-eu-deal-closer-future-ties)
- Speaking in Italy on Friday, Alexander was expected to call the Brussels agreement "a floor – a proof of concept, not a ceiling on our ambitions", and to say that Scotland's place in Europe rests on "interdependence, not independence". These quotes come from pre-released extracts; the speech as delivered has not been checked. [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/26/uk-bill-to-implement-eu-deal-closer-future-ties), [Standard/PA](https://www.standard.co.uk/news/politics/scotland-douglas-alexander-prime-minister-europe-andy-burnham-b1306624.html)

**Claimed or disputed**

- **Tuition fees.** EU diplomats told the Guardian that the agreements "will not materialise" without a deal on home-rate tuition fees for EU students within six months. UK officials "firmly denied" that the two are linked. [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/26/uk-bill-to-implement-eu-deal-closer-future-ties)
- **Influence over EU rules.** The government says British experts will be consulted while EU laws are drafted. Badenoch accused Burnham of seeking "a blank cheque to install and enact EU law". Farage said the deals take Britain back into "much of the European Union's single market". [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/ck2057x387ego)
- **Devolved powers.** Scotland's Europe minister, Stephen Gethins, backed the reset but said powers returning from Brussels must not be "centralised in London". [Standard/PA](https://www.standard.co.uk/news/politics/scotland-douglas-alexander-prime-minister-europe-andy-burnham-b1306624.html)

**Opinion and analysis**

- Lord Mandelson, a former EU trade commissioner, writing in *UnHerd*: the Commission will allow "no derogations" or cherry-picking. He argues that access to the single market without membership would make Britain "a regulatory satellite", and that rejoining would need a mandate at a general election. [UnHerd](https://unherd.com/2026/11/the-reality-of-rejoin/)
- On the right-leaning *ConservativeHome*, Diana Dickinson argues that until advocates show membership would beat the alternatives, Britain should focus on "practical cooperation and the domestic foundations of prosperity". [ConservativeHome](https://conservativehome.com/2026/11/26/diana-dickinson-why-we-must-oppose-rejoining-the-eu/) (feed summary only)

**What next**: The Commons second reading is on 7 December. The youth-mobility treaty text is due "in the coming days".

### Mandelson papers: reshuffle messages, a softened China speech and a Trump-adviser lunch

The third release of Mandelson papers, published on Thursday 26 November, went beyond the shareholding detail reported in the previous update. Ministers responded with promises of reform.

**Established facts**

- In a WhatsApp message to Morgan McSweeney, then Keir Starmer's chief of staff, on 5 September 2025, Mandelson wrote "You should solve your Darren issue and put him at DSIT". Darren Jones was not given that job, and McSweeney replied later that "we seized that day quite well". [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/26/what-we-learned-latest-release-peter-mandelson-documents), [Standard/PA](https://www.standard.co.uk/news/politics/peter-mandelson-keir-starmer-government-olly-robbins-b1306519.html)
- After a Global Counsel director commented on a draft of his Ditchley lecture, Mandelson said he would "soften the lecture on China". In April 2025 he sought an "under the radar" lunch between a Trump economic adviser and a Global Counsel colleague, while he still held shares in the firm. [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/26/peter-mandelson-trump-adviser-meeting-lobbying-firm), [Standard/PA](https://www.standard.co.uk/news/politics/peter-mandelson-andy-burnham-government-prime-minister-b1306549.html)
- Security minister Dan Jarvis told the Commons the government will strengthen its policy on conflicts of interest. Burnham said "there's a lot further to go" on political culture and that the issue would be addressed in his 10-year plan next month. [Standard/PA](https://www.standard.co.uk/news/politics/peter-mandelson-andy-burnham-government-prime-minister-b1306549.html), [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/26/peter-mandelson-trump-adviser-meeting-lobbying-firm)
- Mandelson's conflict-of-interest declaration and the summary of his vetting are still withheld at the request of the Metropolitan Police, which says its misconduct investigation is ongoing. Mandelson was arrested in February, has not been charged and denies wrongdoing. [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/26/peter-mandelson-trump-adviser-meeting-lobbying-firm), [Standard/PA](https://www.standard.co.uk/news/politics/peter-mandelson-andy-burnham-government-prime-minister-b1306549.html)

### Burnham says migration is now under "sufficient" control

Burnham responded on Thursday 26 November to the ONS figure of 77,000 net migration, reported in the previous update.

**Established facts**

- Burnham said there was now "a sufficient degree of control over the system" but that "there is more to be done". Asked whether free movement could return as part of closer EU ties, he said "I don't think you can say that Brexit did anything other than weaken control of immigration". [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/ck792l8ndqyqo), [Standard/PA](https://www.standard.co.uk/news/politics/andy-burnham-prime-minister-brexit-david-cameron-shabana-mahmood-b1306557.html)

**Claimed or disputed**

- Reform UK's Robert Jenrick said the fall "came about solely because I went to see Rishi Sunak" in 2023 and threatened to resign. Farage worried that high-earning taxpayers were leaving. Nobody else quoted backs Jenrick's claim to sole credit. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/ck792l8ndqyqo)

**Opinion and analysis**

- In the *Guardian*, Madeleine Sumption of the Migration Observatory says Labour benefited from conditions the Conservatives did not have: control over EU migration, a revised ONS method that took "tens of thousands off" the figure, and heavy non-EU emigration left over from earlier high arrivals. [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/uk-news/2026/nov/26/how-much-sharp-fall-uk-immigration-labour-policies)

### East Midlands road schemes cut to fund defence

**Established facts**

- The Department for Transport confirmed it has shelved the A38 Derby junctions and the A46 Newark bypass, cutting planned road spending by £428m over four years to "prioritise crucial investment to our national defence". East Midlands mayor Claire Ward (Labour) called the decision "short-sighted" (single source). [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/26/major-east-midlands-road-projects-shelved-to-divert-funding-to-defence)
