## Top stories

### Experts warn that AI labs may have cryptography breakthroughs they have not disclosed

Following Thursday's Bank of England warning, the debate over AI and encryption went mainstream. On Saturday 28 November the *Guardian* led with leading computer scientists saying that frontier AI labs may be sitting on "bombshell" results that could weaken the public-key encryption used by banks and governments. No lab has announced any such result, and no break of deployed cryptography has been publicly verified.

**Established facts**

- On Wednesday 25 November, Scott Aaronson of the University of Texas wrote that he is "virtually certain, from multiple reliable sources, that the AI labs are now sitting on one or more bombshells" in cryptography. In the same post he said he was "much, much less confident" about what they are, and did not want to spread "unverified rumors". [Shtetl-Optimized](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10379), [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/28/ai-artificial-intelligence-encryption-global-chaos)
- The concern follows a run of AI-produced proofs of long-open problems. On 23 November, Anthropic and OpenAI posted proofs of Valiant's VP ≠ VNP conjecture a few hours apart, both with Lean formalisations, and both are still awaiting independent expert confirmation. Aaronson also points to new OpenAI preprints on the matrix-multiplication exponent and on Fourier transforms. [heise](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Math-race-Anthropic-and-OpenAI-offer-near-simultaneous-proofs-of-VP-VNP-11536759.html), [Shtetl-Optimized](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10379)

**Claimed or disputed**

- Nobody has substantiated what the "bombshells" are. Aaronson and others suggest that AI may have found either a classical shortcut that breaks encryption or a proof that no such shortcut exists. The Telegraph reportedly carried a rumour of "factoring in P", meaning an efficient general method for factoring integers that would undermine RSA. Its article could not be opened, and Yahoo Tech says there is no verified evidence for the rumour. [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/28/ai-artificial-intelligence-encryption-global-chaos), [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/cybersecurity/articles/mathematicians-warn-ai-could-soon-192637212.html)

**Opinion and analysis**

- Alan Woodward of the University of Surrey told the *Guardian* that a breakthrough is unlikely to "undo encryption by the end of the week", but that banks and governments "need to be agile". Steven Murdoch of UCL said the post-quantum migration target of about 2032 "was probably going to be missed". [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/28/ai-artificial-intelligence-encryption-global-chaos)
- The mathematician Marcus du Sautoy, speaking on BBC Radio 4, asked why nobody is talking about the core cryptographic problem, and suggested "maybe they've already solved it". [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/28/ai-artificial-intelligence-encryption-global-chaos)
- The FT ran an explainer and an editorial under the headline "AI and the decryption nightmare". The editorial argues that the breakthroughs call "for faster upgrades to security, not panic". [FT](https://www.ft.com/content/4114d053-de5e-46a5-8b46-758726abdcb7) (feed summary only), [FT](https://www.ft.com/content/1a29e62d-b18d-4bad-937d-34f3eae0587a) (feed summary only)

**What next**: Watch for statements from the labs, and for independent verification of the published proofs.

### Hackers breach the US Air Force's Cloud One platform

Reuters reported on Friday 27 November that hackers had breached Cloud One, the US Air Force's main cloud platform, which is built on Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Google services. It is the latest in a string of reported intrusions at US federal agencies.

**Established facts**

- A US official confirmed the breach to NBC News but gave no details of when it happened or what it affected. The Air Force said it "does not comment on the security posture, vulnerabilities, or operational status of specific networks and systems". [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/hackers-attack-penetrate-air-forces-computing-platform-rcna611559), [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/27/hackers-breach-us-air-force-cloud-computing)
- Cloud One handles personnel, pay, logistics and maintenance. It also runs a munitions-tracking system that covers more than 200 sites. [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/27/hackers-breach-us-air-force-cloud-computing)

**Claimed or disputed**

- Reuters, as relayed by the *Guardian* and Bloomberg, cites two sources. They say the breach was found within the past month during an upgrade, caused disruption across the platform, and came through a software vulnerability. Reuters could not establish who the attackers were or whether any data was stolen. The original Reuters article could not be opened. [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/27/hackers-breach-us-air-force-cloud-computing), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-27/hackers-breached-us-air-force-s-cloud-platform)
- NBC's sources say the FBI's National Crime Information Center was also recently breached. A spokesperson for the Office of the Director of National Intelligence told Bloomberg that the reported intrusion at the spy agencies caused "no disruptions to critical capabilities or classified systems". [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/hackers-attack-penetrate-air-forces-computing-platform-rcna611559), [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/27/hackers-breach-us-air-force-cloud-computing)

### SAP confirms a security incident involving its GitHub environment

SAP has confirmed that it detected "suspicious activity" and took emergency measures. It says that so far it has found "no evidence of unauthorized access to customer business data". The incident was first reported by Der Spiegel.

**Established facts**

- SAP confirmed the incident to SecurityWeek and to BleepingComputer, but did not say which systems were affected. [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/sap-confirms-cyberattack-source-code-reportedly-compromised/), [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/sap-confirms-security-incident-after-suspicious-github-activity/)

**Claimed or disputed**

- Der Spiegel, citing insiders, reports that attackers stole employees' authentication tokens and used them to copy and alter source code on GitHub, possibly with AI assistance. Handelsblatt reports that SAP has replaced signing keys and suspended automated development operations. None of the reports say that tampered code reached customers. [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/sap-confirms-cyberattack-source-code-reportedly-compromised/), [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/sap-confirms-security-incident-after-suspicious-github-activity/)

### OpenAI countersues Apple over trade secrets and suppliers

On Friday 27 November OpenAI filed counterclaims in federal court in Northern California, raising the stakes in Apple's July lawsuit over AI devices.

**Established facts**

- The filing accuses Apple of "unlawful, tortious, and unfair business practices". It says Apple's policies on personal iCloud accounts and same-day exits create "trapdoors" that let it accuse departing staff of theft, and that Apple pressured suppliers not to work with OpenAI. Both sides want a jury trial. [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/27/tech/openai-apple-countersuit), [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/27/openai-countersues-apple-alleging-trade-secret-theft-and-supplier-threats/)
- Apple called the counterclaims "a transparent attempt to distract" and said OpenAI "has already been ordered to stop using Apple's confidential information". Apple's case is set for trial on 19 July 2027. [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/27/openai-countersues-apple-alleging-trade-secret-theft-and-supplier-threats/), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/27/tech/openai-apple-countersuit)

**Claimed or disputed**

- OpenAI alleges that a staffer who returned to Apple took confidential files about its devices. Apple says its investigation found no OpenAI confidential information. Neither side's claims have been tested in court. [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/27/openai-countersues-apple-alleging-trade-secret-theft-and-supplier-threats/), [FT](https://www.ft.com/content/af599e07-d44c-4068-ab84-6ee3c85551d4) (feed summary only)
