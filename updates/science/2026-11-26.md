## Top stories

### Riemann hypothesis: AI-assisted proof accepted as verified, but not yet understood

The proof was announced on Friday 20 November, before this window; this week's news is the reaction. The formal proof has been machine-checked, yet no human-readable account of the argument exists, and mathematicians are divided over what that means for the field.

**Established facts**

- Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, released on 20 November a proof developed with the company's Claude model and written in the Lean proof language; hours later Ravi Vakil of Stanford posted a separate, human-written preprint by Alessandro Masullo. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03812-7), [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/riemann-hypothesis-solved-math-ai)
- On 21 November the Lean Focused Research Organization and the Mathlib Initiative said in a joint statement: "We have full confidence that Alpöge's proof of the Riemann Hypothesis is correct." MIT's Andrew Sutherland separately recompiled it. [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-ai-riemann-hypothesis-proof-is-almost-certainly-true-independent-mathematicians-attest/), [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03835-0)
- Alpöge has not released the AI-written explanation of the proof. [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/why-ais-proof-of-the-riemann-hypothesis-is-so-bittersweet/)

**Claimed or disputed**

- The Association for Human Mathematics says no mathematician can "confirm with full certainty that the proof is correct" and that no human can claim responsibility for it. [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/riemann-hypothesis-solved-math-ai)
- Andrew Granville (Montreal) called Alpöge's four-page sketch "garbled" and linked its timing to Anthropic's falling share price after its IPO; Alpöge wrote that this "cynical view" is "false". Anthropic had not responded to Nature. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03812-7)
- The proof's size is reported differently: 17 million lines of Lean code (Nature), "250,000 pages" (Science News); Scientific American says Alpöge later called the page figure a joke. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03835-0), [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/why-ais-proof-of-the-riemann-hypothesis-is-so-bittersweet/)

**Opinion and analysis**

- Caltech PhD student Tasmin Chu, of the Association for Human Mathematics, told Nature her reaction was "grave concern" and called for a pause in AI development; Warwick's Harini Desiraju said the two proofs "could present a great opportunity for discovery". [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03835-0)
- Bryna Kra (Northwestern) stresses it was not AI alone: "There is someone to stand behind" the work. Gary Marcus (NYU) doubts the result generalises beyond mathematics. [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/riemann-hypothesis-solved-math-ai)
- Peter Hall in *Scientific American* writes that the problem "was resolved in a way that, so far, no one can figure out". [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/why-ais-proof-of-the-riemann-hypothesis-is-so-bittersweet/)

**What next**: Expert reading of the Lean proof and of Masullo's preprint; neither is peer reviewed, and the Clay Mathematics Institute's $1 million prize decision is open.

### COP31 ends in Antalya without a cover decision

The UN climate summit closed on Sunday 22 November, two days late, with no agreed overarching statement, the first such failure since Copenhagen in 2009. Analysis and reaction ran through the week.

**Established facts**

- The Turkish-Australian presidency's draft "Antalya pact" did not mention fossil fuels and was rejected by the EU and by Pacific island and Latin American states; the UK also rejected it, according to Carbon Brief. [Ars Technica / Inside Climate News](https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/11/cop31-ends-in-failure-as-countries-split-over-fossil-fuels-climate-finance/), [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/cop31-key-outcomes-agreed-at-the-un-climate-talks-in-antalya), [ABC News (Australia)](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-11-23/cop31-talks-break-down-in-longest-ever-un-climate-debate/107414772)
- Other decisions were adopted, including a just-transition mechanism, and about 80 countries signed a target of 35% of final energy from electricity by 2035. [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/cop31-key-outcomes-agreed-at-the-un-climate-talks-in-antalya)
- The talks ended 44 hours after the scheduled close, the longest COP on record. [ABC News (Australia)](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-11-23/cop31-talks-break-down-in-longest-ever-un-climate-debate/107414772), [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/cop31-key-outcomes-agreed-at-the-un-climate-talks-in-antalya)

**Claimed or disputed**

- The number of objectors varies: Nature reports a coalition of "around 90 countries" and "almost 100 nations" walking out; other reports name the EU and "several" states. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03827-0), [Ars Technica / Inside Climate News](https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/11/cop31-ends-in-failure-as-countries-split-over-fossil-fuels-climate-finance/)
- Saudi negotiator Mohammad Ayoub said the Arab League backed the pact and called opposition to carbon capture unscientific; Ireland's Darragh O'Brien, for the EU, said the text "greenwashes fossil fuels". [Ars Technica / Inside Climate News](https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/11/cop31-ends-in-failure-as-countries-split-over-fossil-fuels-climate-finance/)

**Opinion and analysis**

- A *Nature* editorial (24 November) argues that consensus-based COPs remain essential and that failure can prompt later action. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03810-9)
- Experts told Carbon Brief the talks did not strictly "collapse": ambitious countries rejected "the lowest common denominator on offer". [Carbon Brief](https://www.carbonbrief.org/cop31-key-outcomes-agreed-at-the-un-climate-talks-in-antalya)

### Hera's first close-up images show how DART reshaped asteroid Dimorphos

On Wednesday 25 November ESA released the first images from its Hera spacecraft at the Didymos-Dimorphos system, target of NASA's 2022 deflection test.

**Established facts**

- The struck face of 151-metre Dimorphos is flattened into a stadium-sized crater spanning its width; the asteroid is tumbling, and hundreds of metre-scale boulders surround the pair. [ESA](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/operations/space-safety/hera/hera-reveals-stadium-sized-asteroid-crater-left-after-spacecraft-impact), [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cw8dv112r5gmo)
- Hera is 25-30 km from the asteroids, will approach to about 13 km in December and deploy two CubeSats in January 2027. [ESA](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/operations/space-safety/hera/hera-reveals-stadium-sized-asteroid-crater-left-after-spacecraft-impact)

**Claimed or disputed**

- Whether the boulders are DART ejecta or older debris is unsettled, team scientist Naomi Murdoch said; CubeSat engineers describe them as a navigation risk. [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/11/europes-hera-mission-returns-to-the-chaotic-scene-of-a-cosmic-smashup/)
- Principal investigator Patrick Michel said the team is "still not sure" how a crater that large formed while the rest of the surface looks undisturbed. These are mission-team statements; no paper has been published. [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/11/europes-hera-mission-returns-to-the-chaotic-scene-of-a-cosmic-smashup/), [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/in-2022-nasa-struck-an-asteroid-now-we-are-finally-seeing-the-massive-impact-up-close/)

### China launches Shenzhou-24 with first foreign astronaut bound for Tiangong

**Established facts**

- Shenzhou-24 lifted off from Jiuquan at 08:18 local time on Wednesday 25 November carrying Tang Hongbo, Wu Yi and Pakistani pilot Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, the first foreigner on a Chinese crewed mission; Ali was aboard Tiangong later that day. [DW](https://www.dw.com/en/china-launches-shenzhou-24-to-tiangong-station/a-80042281), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/26/iqbal-in-space-urdu-poets-verses-unite-india-pakistan-beyond-earth)
- Ali, a payload specialist, is due to stay about a week. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03829-y)

**Claimed or disputed**

- The China Manned Space Agency called the launch a "complete success" (official statement, relayed by DW). Accounts differ on astronaut Zhu Yangzhu's extended stay for a human-adaptation study: "another six months" (Nature) or a year in total (DW). [DW](https://www.dw.com/en/china-launches-shenzhou-24-to-tiangong-station/a-80042281), [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03829-y)

### Two of NASA's four Mars missions pause science

**Established facts**

- Perseverance has paused science since a UHF radio fault on 30 October; the backup radio transmits at about half power, and the rover is using a slower direct-to-Earth link. [NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/mars/2026/11/19/nasas-perseverance-odyssey-teams-investigate-technical-issues/), [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/11/half-of-nasas-aging-mars-fleet-knocked-offline-by-technical-issues/)
- A reaction wheel on the 2001 Mars Odyssey orbiter stopped in early November, leaving two working; NASA estimates one to two months of thruster fuel without a third (single source for the fuel estimate). [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/11/half-of-nasas-aging-mars-fleet-knocked-offline-by-technical-issues/)

**What next**: NASA says Perseverance "could return to science by the end of November".
